Office Inbox

What an elected office receives: verified constituents' common ground, their disagreements, who took part, and what it would cost. The office answers in public, reports what happens next, and can ask its district to weigh in.

The inbox belongs to the seat, not the person. Responses are signed by the office and name who approved them. When a new officeholder takes the seat, every open package, request, and past response passes to them, so the record carries over to the next officeholder.

Example data. The office, participants, and votes are invented to show how the inbox works. Responses here are not sent anywhere.
