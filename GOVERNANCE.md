# How Civic Accord is governed

This is the charter: who holds the keys, who can change what, and how the project moves to a neutral host. Like everything else here, each rule says whether it works now or is planned.

## 1. What this project is

Civic Accord is a free, open, nonpartisan tool for residents to work with their governments. It takes no side on any bill, candidate, or party. It is meant to be owned and run by a neutral host, such as a university or a nonpartisan nonprofit, not by any one person or company.

## 2. The core that doesn't change casually

These parts are the project's spine. Changing them follows section 5, never a quiet commit:

- Verified voters only can vote or add statements; everyone can read.
- The consensus rule: a group counts at 10 or more votes, a label needs two groups, common ground is 70% or more of every counted group.
- Costs always say how they were estimated, including cost per resident; unknowns are "not yet estimated."
- The conversation is with an office, not a person. Offices answer in public with a reason.
- AI never touches votes, voter checks, or the official text people vote on.
- Every rule the tool applies cites that government's own law.
- No party labels, ratings of officials, ads, fundraising, or sale of data.

## 3. Who holds the keys

**Works now.** The domain (civicaccord.org), the hosting account, and the GitHub organization are held by the convener, the resident who started the project. They are held in trust for the project, not as personal property.

**Planned.** A second administrator will be added to each account so no single lost device or absence can lock the project out.

**Planned.** Three to five stewards will be named, each from a different organization, including partner groups with different viewpoints. No two stewards may come from the same organization. The convener may be one steward, never more than one vote. Steward names are published in this file.

## 4. The two-steward rule (planned)

Once stewards are named, any of the following needs at least two stewards to agree, recorded in the decision log:

- changing who can sign in to the domain, hosting, or code accounts
- changing the core in section 2
- choosing or changing the host
- accepting money, a partnership, or a data agreement on the project's behalf
- amending this charter

Until stewards are named, the convener makes these decisions alone, records each one in the decision log, and does not accept money or sell anything on the project's behalf.

## 5. Changing this charter (planned)

An amendment is proposed as a public GitHub issue, stays open for 14 days of comment, and then needs two stewards to agree. The change and its reason go in the decision log.

## 6. Moving to a host

When a neutral host agrees to run Civic Accord, the domain, accounts, code, data, and name transfer to the host at no cost. The convener moves to an advisory seat with no account access. The host adopts this charter or publishes its own, and the decision log records the hand-off.

## 7. Conflicts of interest

Anyone making a decision for the project (the convener, a steward, a pack reviewer, a moderator) discloses any stake in the matter and steps aside from that decision. Disclosures are public. The convener has already disclosed stakes in short-term rentals and farming, and takes no part in reviewing packs on housing or agriculture.

## 8. The decision log

Every governance decision is recorded in [GOVERNANCE-LOG.md](GOVERNANCE-LOG.md): the date, the decision, the reason, and who agreed. The log is append-only. Entries are never edited or removed; a mistake is corrected with a new entry.

## 9. What this charter does not do

It does not create a company, a membership, a token, or a treasury. There is no voting share to buy. Deliberation results are advisory and belong to the voters who took part; the project's governance never touches them.
